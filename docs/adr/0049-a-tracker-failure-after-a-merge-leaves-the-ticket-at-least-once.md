# A Tracker failure after a merge leaves the Ticket at-least-once; the report is the compensation

*Narrowed 2026-10 by [ADR 0050](0050-a-blockers-missing-merge-marker-is-a-run-start-disclosure-never-a-gate.md): the deferred detection-at-next-**Run** slice is decided there — a best-effort merge-marker disclosure at **Run** start, never a gate, never a skip.*

#114. The merge and `leaveFrontier` cannot be atomic: ADR 0033 settled that `leaveFrontier` runs after the merge, because the Landing it reports does not exist before it — and the two effects cannot be made all-or-nothing without either reopening that ordering or building two-phase Tracker writes. The first trades this failure for the worse one ADR 0033 rejected: a Ticket off the Frontier with nothing landed. The second adds a second Tracker call per Ticket and fails into the same window anyway, since a crash between the phases is the same at-least-once with more machinery. So the sequence has exactly one step where a failure leaves landed work still on the Frontier, and the decision is to declare that rather than hide it: a Ticket whose merge succeeded and whose `leaveFrontier` then threw — the shipped Adapters' or a Consumer's override — has landed, and the Run stops with a tracker-stage hard stop whose report names the Ticket, its merge commit, and the Run Branch, and says the Ticket is still on the Frontier and must be taken off by hand before continuing. This is the one deliberate exception to the property the rest of the loop maintains — that a stop leaves the Frontier holding precisely the unfinished work, which is why ReadyRun needs no resume state. Naming the window in the report follows the handoff-is-a-report stance (ADR 0033) and the #112 precedent that a stop which did work says how to continue from it: it converts a silent trap into an instruction, the same shape #104 asks of a merge failure.

Detection at the next Run — passing over a picked Ticket whose merge commit is already reachable from the base, keyed on the deterministic merge message — is deferred, not rejected. It only helps the `--base` continuation, since a fresh base cannot see the merge at all; any pass-over must first be reconciled with ADR 0013's refusal of the word "skip", and it is a different thing wearing it; and the first-parent-walking machinery lives on the Delivery side (ADR 0048), where deciding a second matcher beside #70's still-open blocking semantics risks two inconsistent readers of one history. Retry of `leaveFrontier` is #113's filed question and narrows the window without closing it; this decision stands whatever it answers.

## Consequences

- A tracker-stage hard stop splits in two by what exists when it fires: a Tracker failure anywhere before the Landing exists reports as it does today; a failure at `leaveFrontier` after a successful merge must additionally name the landed Ticket, its merge commit, the Run Branch, and the manual step.
- The Consumer override path gets the same report: it is the same handoff failing.
- "One Ticket is one entry in the Run Branch's first-parent history" holds on the git side and is violated on the Tracker side inside this window; the report is the reconciliation of record until detection is decided.
- Revisit detection together with #70 (Frontier and blocking semantics) and the Delivery reconciliation machinery, not beside them.
